Mission Context & Objective

Post-Fire Soil & Canopy Degradation Assessment

In late Summer 2024, the Sierra Creek Wildfire swept through critical conifer stands in the Stanislaus National Forest, threatening hydrologic basins and sensitive watershed boundaries. Immediate post-fire salvage operations and soil erosion stabilization teams required rapid, geometrically consistent burn severity maps.

To eliminate cloud distortion and terrain shadow artifacts, an automated spatial pipeline was constructed using Google Earth Engine (GEE). Surface reflectance products from ESA’s Sentinel-2 constellation were pulled across two calibrated temporal windows: a pre-fire baseline (July 15–30, 2024) and an immediate post-fire window (September 18–25, 2024).

By isolating the Near-Infrared spectrum (sensitive to chlorophyllic cell walls) and Short-Wave Infrared II spectrum (sensitive to water content and ash charring), the delta Normalized Burn Ratio (dNBR) model precisely delineated severe canopy incinerations from moderate scorch patterns.

Cartographic Insight

High-slope aspects (>35°) in the central canyon manifested extreme severity plumes due to topographical chimney convection, precipitating immediate secondary debris flow hazards modeled for local Forest Service engineers.

function maskS2clouds(image) {
  var qa = image.select('QA60');
  var cloudBitMask = 1 << 10;
  var cirrusBitMask = 1 << 11;
  var mask = qa.bitwiseAnd(cloudBitMask).eq(0)
    .and(qa.bitwiseAnd(cirrusBitMask).eq(0));
  return image.updateMask(mask).divide(10000);
}
